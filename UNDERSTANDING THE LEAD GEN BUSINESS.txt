Let's break down every part.

1. What exactly is a lead?

A lead is a person or business that has shown interest in buying a service.

For your business:

A restaurant in Sandton has a commercial espresso machine that stopped heating and wants someone to repair it.

That restaurant is a lead.

For example, someone visits your website and submits:

Business: ABC Café
Location: Sandton
Machine: La Marzocco espresso machine
Problem: Not heating
Phone: 071 XXX XXXX
Urgency: Today

That's a lead.

But not every lead is valuable.

Someone could submit:

"How much does a coffee machine cost?"

That's an enquiry, but it may not be a repair lead.

Someone who says:

"My commercial Rancilio machine is leaking and my restaurant can't operate. I need a technician today."

That's a high-value lead.

2. Qualified vs unqualified leads

This is extremely important because technicians don't want to pay you for garbage enquiries.

🟢 Qualified lead

A qualified lead should generally contain:

Real customer
Real contact details
Commercial machine
Repair/service requirement
Location within your service area
Specific problem
Customer actually wants a technician

Example:

Restaurant: Café XYZ
Location: Sandton
Machine: Nuova Simonelli
Problem: No water coming through
Phone: 071 XXX XXXX
Request: Technician today

That's a qualified lead.

🔴 Unqualified lead

Examples:

"How much is a coffee machine?"

"I need a job."

"Do you sell coffee?"

Fake phone number

Person outside Johannesburg

Domestic machine when your technician only accepts commercial equipment

Student asking for information

These shouldn't normally be charged as qualified leads.

7. Pay-per-lead vs commission vs monthly fee

Let's compare.

Model	How you get paid	Risk	Difficulty
Pay-per-lead	Fixed amount per lead	Low	Easy
Commission	% of completed jobs	Medium	Medium
Monthly fee	Fixed monthly amount	Low once established	Harder to sell
Hybrid	Combination	Low/medium	Best long-term
My recommendation for you

Pay-per-qualified-lead

Example:

You send the same customer to:

Technician B
Technician C

You can charge less.

Which should you use?

For your business:

It creates a much better relationship with technicians.

Imagine paying R400 for a lead and then discovering four other technicians received exactly the same customer.

The technician won't be happy.

23. What could cause the business to fail?

This is VERY important.

❌ Problem 1 — No technicians

You generate 20 leads.

Nobody can service them.

Your reputation gets destroyed.

Solution:

Build technician coverage before aggressively advertising.

❌ Problem 2 — Fake/low-quality leads

Technicians receive:

"How much does a machine cost?"

They don't make money.

They stop buying your leads.

Solution:

Qualify every lead.

❌ Problem 3 — Technicians don't pay

You generate leads.

They avoid payment.

Solution:

Contracts + tracking + lead IDs + payment records.

❌ Problem 4 — Website doesn't rank

You spend weeks building the site.

Nobody visits.

Solution:

SEO strategy + local pages + useful content + Google Business Profile + eventually paid advertising.

❌ Problem 5 — You pretend to be technicians

This can destroy trust.

Don't say:

"Our technicians..."

unless you actually have a network operating under your business.

Instead:

"We connect businesses with independent commercial coffee-machine technicians."

That's much safer and more honest.

❌ Problem 6 — One technician controls everything

If one person receives all your leads and leaves:

Your business collapses.

Solution:

Multiple technicians.

❌ Problem 7 — You sell the same lead to everyone

Technicians become angry.

Solution:

Start with exclusive leads.

13. Scale

The most dangerous mistake would be spending weeks creating a beautiful website and SEO pages before finding out whether technicians are actually willing to pay you for leads.

Your first objective isn't:

"Build a website."

It's:

"Prove that I can generate a customer and get a technician to pay me for that customer."

Once you can do that 10 times, you have a business model worth scaling.



Here's how to price it without losing money or scaring them off:

1. Understand what they make per job

Domestic (home De'Longhi, Nespresso, etc): Call-out R450-R750 + repair R800-R1,500
Commercial (restaurant, office in Sandton): Call-out R750-R1,200 + repair R2,500-R8,000 + ongoing service contract
If they close 1 out of 3 leads, they still make profit. Your price should be about 10-15% of one job.

2. Don't charge R50 like everyone else does
Plumber leads in JHB go for R80-R150 because there are 500 plumbers fighting. Coffee machine techs? There's maybe 10 guys covering Sandton. You have leverage.

Start here:

Pay-Per-Lead (best for you starting out)

Domestic leads: R180 - R250 per lead
Commercial leads: R300 - R450 per lead
Give them the first 2 leads free to prove quality, then prepaid bundle: "10 leads for R2,000" - you get cash upfront.
Pay-Per-Booking (if you want more)
If you actually book the appointment for them: R350 - R500 per booked job. Much more valuable, techs love it because they don't have to chase.

Commission (only if you trust them)
15-20% of the first invoice. Risky in SA - many won't report honestly.

3. My recommendation for you in Sandton:
Start with R200 per domestic / R350 per commercial, prepaid.

Say this on the call:

"I generate people in Sandton whose coffee machine is broken right now. I don't do monthly fees. You only pay when I send you a name, number, and what machine is broken. If it’s fake, I replace it free. Most guys close 1 in 2 and make R1,500+ per job. Want me to send you 2 for free tomorrow to test?"

That way:

You don't undercharge (R200 is fair, not cheap)
You don't overcharge (R350 is still cheap vs R3k job)
Free test kills their objection
Once you have 2-3 techs paying, raise new clients to R250/R400.
